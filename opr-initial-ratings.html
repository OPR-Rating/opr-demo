Starting Ratings & Levels, Explained

How your players get their first number — and why it quickly becomes the right one.

1 · The starting scale

Every player begins with an initial rating — a starting point on OPR's scale, set once when they join. The working ranges:

Ladies900–10501050–12001200–13501350–1500

2 · The simple route — one default for everyone

Don't want to grade every member on day one? Use the suggested defaults:

Men — 1500    Ladies — 1300

Start everyone there and simply let them play. This is how most clubs begin.

Why this works: OPR recalculates ratings with every match — and it scores how you played (set margins and games), not just whether you won. A player who starts too high loses rating quickly to those beating them; a player who starts too low climbs just as fast. Within a handful of matches, the numbers separate and settle around each player's true level — the starting point simply stops mattering.

3 · The bespoke route — grade your own players

If your club knows its players, you can set each member's initial rating individually using the ranges in the table above — your coach or committee simply places each player in a band. OPR's bulk member upload takes a per-player starting rating, so a graded list from a spreadsheet loads in one go. Bespoke seeding means the very first ladder already looks sensible; the engine then fine-tunes from there.

Either way, you can't get it "wrong." Initial ratings are a starting point, not a verdict — the match engine does the real grading, match after match.

4 · From rating to Level

OPR shows each player and pair a Level — simply the rating divided by 200. It's the friendly headline number used across ladders and tournaments:

5 · Comparing with Playtomic (and similar apps)

Many players already know their Playtomic level and want to know where they sit on OPR. The two systems measure differently — OPR's scale is wider and doubles-first — but as a practical rule of thumb:

OPR Level ≈ Playtomic level + 5.5 to 6

So a Playtomic 4.0 typically sits around OPR Level 9.5–10, and an OPR Level 7.5 player is broadly a Playtomic 1.5–2. It's a guide, not a conversion — no two rating systems map exactly — but it gives your members an immediate feel for their number. OPR works happily alongside Playtomic: book your courts there, and let OPR run your club's own ratings, ladders and tournaments.
