Understanding OPR

Starting Ratings & Levels

How your players' first ratings are set, how the system settles itself, and how an OPR level compares to the apps you already use.

Every player needs a starting rating — a sensible first guess at their standard. It doesn't need to be perfect. From the first match, OPR refines it based on how each player actually performs, so the ratings quickly find their own true level.

Where every player begins

OPR uses two broad starting bands that reflect the reality of club play:

Men

1000 – 1800
inexperienced → advanced

Ladies

900 – 1500
inexperienced → advanced

You place each member somewhere in their band based on what you already know about their standard.

The easy default — start in the middle

If you'd rather not rate each player individually, the simplest and perfectly valid approach is to start everyone at the middle of their band:

Suggested men's start

1500 · level 7.5

Suggested ladies' start

1300 · level 6.5

Why the system quickly sorts itself out

The starting number is only a seed. From the very first match, OPR adjusts each rating based on how you actually play:

›

Beating a stronger pair moves you up more than beating a weaker one.

›

Winning convincingly counts for more than scraping through.

›

The more you play, the more the rating trusts the result.

So even if two players both start at 1500, within a handful of matches the stronger one rises and the other settles lower — the ratings find their own level naturally. A slightly-off starting guess is corrected by real results, not carried forever. That's why "everyone in the middle" works: play does the sorting.

Prefer to set your own? You can.

OPR is club-owned — nothing is fixed by us. If your coaches or committee know the players well, you're free to evaluate and set each member's starting rating yourself, anywhere within (or beyond) the bands. Bespoke starting ratings simply give the system a head start — it will still refine them with every match.

From rating to level

Alongside the rating, each player has a simpler Level that's easier to talk about — a direct translation of the rating:

Level = Rating ÷ 200 (to one decimal). A player on 1500 is level 7.5; on 1300, level 6.5.

How OPR compares to Playtomic & other apps

Booking apps like Playtomic, MATCHi and Padel Mates use a 0–7 scale. OPR's level runs on a wider club scale, so the numbers don't read the same — but they line up predictably. As a working guide:

Playtomic level ≈ OPR level − 5.5 to 6. An OPR level 7.5 player sits around Playtomic 1.5–2.0; an OPR 8.5 around Playtomic 2.5–3.0.

Treat this as a bridge, not an exact conversion — the two systems measure different populations in different ways. OPR rates you within your club and the people you actually play with, which is what makes it meaningful on your courts. It works alongside the booking apps, never against them.
